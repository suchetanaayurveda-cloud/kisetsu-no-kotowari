## 2. CORE CONCEPT

**Tagline:** 季節を知れば、身体の声が聞こえてくる。
*("When you understand the seasons, you can hear the voice of your body.")*

**One-sentence pitch:**
An Ayurveda physician trained in India and a Japanese cooking teacher co-teach a 3-session online seminar exploring how Ayurvedic wisdom — re-translated through Japan's 24 Solar Terms and food culture — can help modern Japanese people live in harmony with the seasons.

**The intellectual core:**
Ayurveda originated in India's dry continental climate and was built around Indian ingredients, seasons, and lifestyle. Most Ayurveda taught in Japan imports that knowledge as-is, which creates a disconnect. This seminar takes a different stance:

> "Ayurveda is not 'Indian' — it is universal wisdom for living in harmony with nature. That is precisely why it must be *translated* into the language of Japan's climate, food, and seasons rather than transplanted wholesale."

The 二十四節気 (24 Solar Terms) — a traditional East Asian calendar that divides the year into 24 named periods of ~15 days each — serves as the "map" through which Japan's seasonal changes are read. Overlaid with Ayurvedic principles, this reveals that much of Japan's traditional food wisdom (why vinegar in summer, why root vegetables in autumn, why miso in winter) has deep Ayurvedic logic embedded in it, even if Japanese people never named it that way.

## 3. TARGET AUDIENCE

**Primary:** Japanese women and men, roughly 30–55, who:
- Experience seasonal health fluctuations (fatigue in summer, dry skin in autumn, weight gain in winter, heaviness in rainy season) and have accepted them as unavoidable
- Are interested in natural health, food as medicine, or holistic wellness
- May have heard of Ayurveda but find it "too Indian" or inaccessible
- Are already interested in Japanese food culture, fermentation, or traditional cooking
- Want *understanding*, not just recipes — they want to know *why*, not just what

**Secondary:**
- People who already study Ayurveda and want a Japan-specific application lens
- Cooking teachers, health practitioners, wellness professionals seeking new frameworks

**Pain points targeted on the LP:**
- "Every rainy season I get heavy and swollen"
- "I'm exhausted all summer and lose my appetite"
- "I eat healthily but I don't know what's actually right for me"
- "I read health information but can't tell what applies to me"
- "I've been blaming my age, but maybe it's something else"

## 12. COMPETITIVE POSITIONING

This seminar is differentiated by:

1. **Medical legitimacy** — Lead instructor holds BAMS (India's national Ayurveda degree). Not self-taught, not a domestic private school certificate.
2. **Japan-specificity** — Explicitly designed for Japan's climate, ingredients, and seasons. Not adapted-from-India content.
3. **Food as the entry point** — Ayurveda via Japanese food culture, not via body types and Sanskrit terminology.
4. **二十四節気 as the framework** — A Japanese framework most target users already have some familiarity with, making Ayurveda more approachable.
5. **Two-instructor format** — Physician + cooking teacher = both the "why" and the practical "how" covered authentically.
